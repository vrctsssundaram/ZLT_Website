A block can simulate correctly and still be expensive to reuse.

The audit looks at the engineering asset as the next integrator will experience it: code quality, crossing assumptions, interface contracts, clocks and resets, parameterization, constraints, documentation and synthesis observations.

The goal is not to generate a long tool log. It is to decide what can block reuse, what creates avoidable risk and what should be fixed first.

What we inspect

Find the hidden cost before hand-off.

Internal reusable RTL, research-grade blocks, inherited IP and customer-owned designs can all be candidates.

CODE

Structural quality

Lint, coding consistency, maintainability and obvious synthesis or reuse concerns.

CDC/RDC

Crossing assumptions

Clock/reset domains, synchronization strategy, exceptions and areas that need closer review.

CONTRACT

Integration clarity

Interfaces, clocks, resets, parameters, constraints and documentation needed by the next team.

Common questions

Know what the audit is—and what it is not.

What RTL is suitable?

Internal reusable blocks, research-grade RTL, inherited IP and customer-owned designs can all be reviewed.

What does the audit inspect?

Lint/code quality, CDC/RDC, interfaces, clocks/resets, parameters, constraints, documentation and synthesis observations.

Does this replace functional verification?

No. It is a front-end quality and integration-readiness review, not a substitute for block-specific verification or physical sign-off.

What does the audit return?

Prioritized findings, integration gaps and a recommended remediation sequence.

Can Zepto Logic fix the findings?

Yes. RTL, documentation, verification or FPGA follow-on work can be scoped separately.
