C-DOT Samarth: taking zero-knowledge proof acceleration into an FPGA hardware programme.

Public programme proof

Samarth Cohort-II · final 5 from 100+ applicants · Stage-II grant · Demo Day, New Delhi · 12 March 2026.

A programme built around compute-intensive zero-knowledge proof hardware.

Zepto Logic entered C-DOT Samarth Cohort-II with hardware acceleration work in the zk-SNARK / zero-knowledge proof domain. The programme advanced through competitive selection, sponsor review, grant support and FPGA-oriented engineering milestones.

The public story is deliberately focused on programme facts and hardware domain context. Detailed architecture, implementation methods and performance characterization remain protected technical material.

Final 5 from 100+

Zepto Logic was selected among the final five participants from more than one hundred applicants in C-DOT Samarth Cohort-II.

Stage-II grant

The programme received Stage-II grant support as it progressed through the sponsor-reviewed development track.

Demo Day · 12 March 2026

Zepto Logic participated in the C-DOT Samarth Demo Day in New Delhi.

Programme progression

From modelling and RTL into FPGA implementation.

The programme created a structured route for moving mathematically intensive proof-generation work toward reusable hardware engineering.

Phase I

Modelling, Python reference work, RTL development and verification completed. Sponsor verification for Phase I is complete.

Phase II

FPGA optimisation and Zynq UltraScale+ integration work completed and delivered. Sponsor board-level validation is in progress.

Continuing direction

Research continues around reusable arithmetic and transform-oriented accelerator building blocks for zero-knowledge and adjacent cryptographic workloads.

Why this matters

Proof generation concentrates work into kernels that are natural candidates for hardware acceleration.

Multi-scalar multiplication, polynomial transforms, finite-field arithmetic and data movement are examples of compute-intensive areas in modern proof systems. Zepto Logic uses FPGA development to evaluate where dedicated hardware can create a practical implementation path.

Public disclosure stops at the programme and application level. Proprietary architecture, patent-candidate methods and detailed performance data are exchanged only through the appropriate confidential process.
